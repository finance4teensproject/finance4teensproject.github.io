Why Use Financial Calculators?

Reading about financial concepts is only the first step. Using calculators helps you see how different decisions affect your money. For example, increasing your monthly savings by just a small amount can make a significant difference over time because of compound interest.

As you continue learning, return to these tools whenever you want to explore different financial situations or compare different choices.

Try These Scenarios

Think through each situation before using the calculators.

Scenario 1

You earn $120 each month by babysitting. You want to save for a $600 laptop. If you save half of your earnings each month, how long will it take to reach your goal?

Scenario 2

You have monthly expenses totaling $85 and monthly income of $100. Is your budget balanced? How much money can you save?

Scenario 3

Two savings accounts both start with $500. One earns simple interest and the other earns compound interest. Which account will have more money after several years?

Scenario 4

You borrow $2,000. How does increasing the interest rate affect the total amount you repay?

Financial Flashcards

Review important vocabulary from each lesson. Understanding these terms will make it easier to make informed financial decisions.

Budget

A plan for how you will spend and save your money over a specific period of time.

Emergency Fund

Money set aside to pay for unexpected expenses, such as medical bills or car repairs.

Credit Score

A number that represents how responsibly someone has managed borrowed money.

Compound Interest

Interest earned on both the original amount of money and previously earned interest.

APR

Annual Percentage Rate. The yearly cost of borrowing money, including interest and certain fees.

FAFSA

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid used to determine eligibility for financial aid.

Grant

Financial aid that usually does not have to be repaid.

Net Price

The amount a student actually pays for college after scholarships and grants are applied.

Real-Life Challenges

Apply what you've learned by thinking through these financial situations.

💵 Challenge 1

You earn $40 each week. You want to save for a $160 pair of headphones while still having money to spend with friends.

How would you divide your money each week?

📱 Challenge 2

Your phone screen suddenly breaks, and replacing it will cost $180.

How could having an emergency fund help in this situation?

🎓 Challenge 3

College A costs $42,000 each year but offers $24,000 in scholarships.

College B costs $30,000 each year but offers only $8,000 in scholarships.

Which college has the lower net price?

💳 Challenge 4

You receive your first credit card with a $500 limit.

What habits would help you build a strong credit history?
